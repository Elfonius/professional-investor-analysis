## Bottom line

Aster is a fictional decentralized GPU-inference marketplace with real-looking usage and a token that captures little of the value it creates. Paid demand is growing, but 41% of revenue comes from ten customers, emissions are 28% of float, and a 6.2% unlock lands in 45 days. The screen is a **watch**, not a buy: the product case is stronger than the instrument case.

## 1. Business and product

Aster routes inference requests to consumer GPUs and charges a 12% take rate. It reports 1,200 paid accounts, 38% paid demand and 54% gross margin. The top ten customers produce 41% of revenue, and two of them are affiliated with the founding team. The product is useful; the demand base is not yet diversified. The team has shipped for three years, but the CTO is the only person who has maintained the scheduler in production. Evidence tags: [K1] usage dashboard, [K2] customer concentration.

## 2. Instrument and economics

The ASTER token is a staking and payment unit, not equity. It has no claim on revenue, no governance right over the treasury, and no contractual buyback. Supply is 1.46B, float is 438M (30%), FDV is $62.0M and market cap is $18.4M. Annual emission is 28% of float; the next unlock is 6.2% of float in 45 days. A buyback programme exists, but the treasury funds it; it is not funded by operating cash flow. At this price the token is a leveraged bet on narrative, not a claim on the business.

## 3. Valuation, risk and exit

A revenue-multiple approach is not defensible without audited revenue, so the range is scenario-based: bear $0.026, base $0.045, bull $0.071. The base case implies +7%, but the bear case is -38%; the risk-adjusted expected value is unattractive. The three critical risks are emission-driven dilution, customer concentration, and exit depth: ±2% depth is about $180k, and a $500k exit would move the price by high single digits. Technical context is neutral; it is not part of the thesis.

## Verdict

**Watch.** The screen would become constructive only if paid demand exceeds 60% for two consecutive quarters, the 6.2% unlock is absorbed with ±2% depth above $400k, and the buyback is funded by operating cash flow rather than the treasury. A governance or key-person failure would reverse the decision immediately. Legal eligibility, audited revenue and the cap table were not verified; this limits the verdict to a screen.
